No Vendor Lock-In

August 20, 2026

What Vendor Lock-In Actually Means

Imagine you invested in Philips Hue lights, a Sonos speaker system, and a specific brand's thermostat. They all work great—until you realize they don't really work together without paying for yet another hub or app.

Now imagine wanting to switch: you decide Nanoleaf lights are better, but your system doesn't support them. So you rip everything out and start over with a different ecosystem entirely. Your old gear becomes worthless.

That's vendor lock-in: you're locked into one company's way of doing things, and switching costs time, money, and frustration.

Konihaus Works With Everything

Konihaus supports the open standards:

  • Zigbee — lights, switches, sensors (Ikea Tradfri, Tuya, Innr, many others)
  • Z-Wave — locks, thermostats, sensors (Aeotec, Fibaro, Shenzhen)
  • Matter — the new industry standard (coming from Apple, Google, Amazon, others)
  • WiFi — anything that speaks HTTP/MQTT (smart plugs, cameras, custom devices)

This means you're not buying into a "Konihaus ecosystem." You're buying your own smart home that happens to be coordinated by Konihaus.

Mix and Match

You can:

  • Start with Zigbee lights and a Z-Wave thermostat
  • Add a Matter-compatible camera later
  • Swap out a light brand without rebuilding anything
  • Use devices from 10 different manufacturers in the same system

They all talk to the same local hub, follow the same automation logic, appear in the same interface.

Real Example

Let's say you have:

  • 5× Ikea Tradfri bulbs (Zigbee)
  • 1× Aeotec dimmer (Z-Wave)
  • 1× Tado thermostat (WiFi)
  • 1× Eve camera (Matter)

All controlled by one Konihaus setup. One year later, you want to upgrade the bulbs. You pull out the Tradfri bulbs, plug in Innr bulbs (also Zigbee)—and nothing breaks. Your automations still work. The new bulbs slot in seamlessly.

Try doing that with Google Home. It won't work. Each ecosystem is rigid.

You Own the Data

Because Konihaus uses open standards and stores everything locally, your setup is portable. If you ever leave Konihaus:

  • Your devices still exist and work
  • Your data (automations, schedules, history) is readable and portable
  • You can switch to another platform or hand it off to a friend

You're not hostage to a single company's decisions, pricing changes, or business failures.

Why This Matters

Vendor lock-in forces you to:

  • Stay with one brand even if a better option exists
  • Pay premium prices (no competition)
  • Live with suboptimal integrations
  • Throw away perfectly good hardware when you want to try something new

Open standards mean:

  • Real choice
  • Better prices (competition)
  • Innovation from multiple vendors
  • Your investment is protected

The smart home market is fragmented because vendors prefer lock-in. Konihaus refuses to play that game.
